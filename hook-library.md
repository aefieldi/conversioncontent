# The Hook Library

Every hook I use, sorted by what the video is about. 630 hooks. Hooks are formats: keep the wording, fill the brackets and blanks with the topic. Three jobs: the title hook is the text on screen, the verbal hook is the first sentence out of my mouth, the re-hook comes 3 to 5 seconds in without a pause and runs into the first beat. A re-hook can also be the very first thing I say (see Part 4).

## How to use this library

1. Read the video idea or transcript and decide what the video is about. Use the Quick finder below to pick its home section (one of 12).
2. Opening line (verbal hook): pull from the home section's verbal hooks, or open with one of its re-hooks marked "opener".
3. Title hook (text on screen): pull from a section listed under "Pairs with", so the eye and the ear get two different reasons to stay. Use that section's ready-made title hooks or its title formulas (Part 3).
4. Re-hook (3 to 5 seconds in): pull from the home section's re-hooks. Never the same one that opened the video.
5. YouTube titles: pull from the frameworks named in the home section (Part 5).
6. If a section runs short, use its "Pairs with" sections next. Never write a new hook.

## Part 1. How a hook works

**Five rules.**

1. Say something different from what's on screen. The title hook is the text, the verbal hook is the first sentence out of your mouth. If they match, the viewer reads it, hears it, and leaves. Eye and ear each get a reason to stay.
2. Title hook: under 5 words, one word in CAPS, an emoji if it fits. Top or middle of the frame so the caption doesn't cover it. Dates and numbers create urgency.
3. Re-hook at 3 to 5 seconds, without pausing. Mid-sentence, as if you forgot to finish the thought. It re-earns the attention the hook bought and runs straight into the first beat.
4. Every hook triggers one of three feelings. Curiosity (I need to know), fear (I don't want to get this wrong), desire (I want that). The best ones hit two.
5. Pass the stranger test. Someone with no idea what you do should understand the hook instantly. No jargon, no setup, no "in this video I'm going to".

**Pairing.** Pick the verbal hook from the home section that matches the video. Pick the title hook from a different angle, so the eye and the ear get two reasons to stay. The caption is the third angle: the point from the viewer's side, never the hook again.

**The ten principles behind every hook.**

- Curiosity: an open loop they need closed ("what happens to…", "do X really…")
- Desire: what they want: clients, money, freedom, being seen
- Negativity: warnings and problems grab faster than benefits
- Controversy: a counterintuitive or polarising take
- Authority: a name, a credential, a number of clients
- Specificity: numbers, timeframes, exact examples feel real
- Lists: "4 leaks", "5 signs", "3 moves" feel scannable
- Constraint: "the ONE thing", "this ONE mistake"
- Contrast: "not X, but Y", "same gift, different income"
- Speed / ease: "in 30 minutes", "in one sitting", "without posting daily"

**Dial it to 11, then pull back one notch.** Start: "Your tiers are priced wrong" Up: "Why nobody upgrades in your community" Up: "The #1 reason nobody upgrades" Up: "This pricing mistake is killing your upgrades" Too far: "Your pricing is a crime scene" → pull back one

**Universalise, or narrow on purpose.** "Five pricing myths every astrologer believes" filters the audience; "Five pricing myths" is universal. Every word after the hook is a filter. Narrow only when the callout IS the strategy: "Libra Rising" and "Projectors" narrow on purpose, because the right person self-identifies hard.

## Part 3. Title hook formulas (the text on screen)

**Five rules.**

1. Under 5 words. Short and simple.
2. One word in CAPS. The keyword, not the whole line.
3. Say something DIFFERENT out loud than what the title says. If the title is "4 leaks in your business", the first words out of your mouth are not "here are four leaks".
4. Top or middle of the frame. The caption covers the bottom.
5. Emojis and dates help. "Do this before 11/11" outperforms "Do this".


Formula, then an example after the colon.

- T1. [Callout] [emoji] + the gap: 'Libra Rising ♎️ the missing piece'
- T2. [Thing] is a SYMPTOM / isn't [what they think]: 'Clarity is a SYMPTOM', 'Winging it isn't INTUITION'
- T3. Why [who] [bad outcome]: 'Why nobody UPGRADES', 'Why members LEAVE'
- T4. [X] ≠ [Y] / [X] vs [Y]: 'Followers ≠ income', 'Scripts vs FRAMEWORKS'
- T5. Stop [thing] / Stop [thing] NOW: 'Stop selling by the HOUR'
- T6. Number + noun: '4 LEAKS in your business', '3 moves, 90 days'
- T7. Inside / BTS: 'Inside a 60-min AUDIT', 'Audit prep nobody SEES'

## Part 5. Title frameworks (YouTube and long-form)

Built for long-form titles, and the same frameworks work for a reel title, a carousel cover, a post headline or an ad. Match the piece to the framework that amplifies it, generate ten, keep the strongest three.

**Seven rules.**

1. Front-load the search keyword in the first 3 to 5 words.
2. 7 to 11 words, under 55 characters. Mobile truncates after that.
3. Use the exact phrase they'd type at 11pm when they're stuck.
4. One power word per title. Capitalise at most one word.
5. A bracket adds a second promise: (Step by Step), (Without Burnout), (For Readers).
6. Label each title Search (they typed it) or Browse (they scrolled into it). Make some of each.
7. No year unless it's genuinely time-sensitive. No clickbait, no salesy words.

**Title and thumbnail never say the same thing.** Repeats: Title: "Five pricing myths" · Thumb: "Five pricing myths". Complements: Title: "Five pricing myths" · Thumb: "You're doing it wrong". Title asks, thumbnail hints. Title promises, thumbnail adds urgency. Title states the problem, thumbnail shows the feeling.
